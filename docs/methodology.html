Methodology

What this data is, where it comes from, and — just as important — what it leaves out.

Last updated 4 Sep 2026

Where the data comes from

Two official sources, scraped daily and never edited by hand:

  • Senate — the Electronic Financial Disclosure system at efdsearch.senate.gov, which publishes Periodic Transaction Reports as HTML.
  • House — the Clerk of the House at disclosures-clerk.house.gov, which publishes an annual index plus one PDF per report.

Every row on this site links back to the filing it came from. If our reading of a document disagrees with the document, the document is right.

The 45-day rule

The STOCK Act of 2012 requires members of Congress to disclose covered transactions within 45 days. That is the entire basis of this site's timing data, and it has one consequence worth stating plainly: this is transparency on a delay, not a live feed. By the time a trade appears here, it may be six weeks old. Trades disclosed past the deadline are marked ⚠ late.

Why the amounts are ranges

The law only requires a bracket — "$1,001 – $15,000", "$50,001 – $100,000" — never an exact figure. So there is no exact figure to show you. Where we total a member's activity we add the floors and the ceilings separately and present the result as a range. A small number of filings disclose only a floor with no upper bound; those are shown as, for example, "$15,001+", and we never invent the missing ceiling.

Who is not covered

This site covers Congress only — the 535 members of the House and Senate, plus their spouses and dependent children, because those are the people the two sources above publish. Several groups people expect to find here are not covered, and it is not an oversight:

  • The President, Vice President and the executive branch. They do not file with the Senate or the House Clerk. Executive branch officials disclose through the Office of Government Ethics, a separate system with separate forms and its own publication process. Nothing about a president's trading appears in the data this site reads, so no amount of scraping these two sources would surface it.
  • The Supreme Court and the federal judiciary. Justices and judges file annual financial disclosure reports through the Administrative Office of the U.S. Courts — annual reports, not the periodic transaction reports that make near-real-time tracking possible.
  • Federal agency staff and the Federal Reserve. Again a different disclosure regime, not published where we look.

Covering any of those would mean a second data pipeline against a different source, not a change to this one. Until that exists, treating a silence here as "this person did not trade" would be a mistake: it only means this source has nothing to say about them.

What this dataset misses

  • Paper filings are omitted. Reports filed on paper and scanned as images are not machine-readable and do not appear here. They are a minority, but they are not nothing.
  • Most rows have no ticker. Roughly two thirds of disclosed transactions are municipal bonds, structured notes, mutual funds or private holdings with no ticker symbol. They are shown, but they have no per-symbol page.
  • Asset names arrive damaged. Extraction from the source PDFs leaves metadata fragments embedded in asset names. We repair the known patterns. Where the real name was destroyed upstream, we show the ticker and link the original filing rather than guess at a name.
  • Party affiliation is looked up, not derived. It comes from a hand-maintained list. A member we cannot match with certainty shows "Party unknown" — we do not guess party from a name.
  • Rolling window. The site covers the last 120 days of filings, not the full history.
  • A disclosure is not a trade you can see. Filings report the trade date and a bracket, not a price, a counterparty, or a reason.

Who actually made the trade

Filings distinguish the member from a spouse, a dependent child, or a jointly held account. We show that in the Owner field. Many transactions attributed to a member were executed by a spouse or a managed trust the member may not direct.
