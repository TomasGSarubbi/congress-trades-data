Replicable from Argentina

Which of the trades disclosed by the US Congress can actually be bought on the Buenos Aires exchange — and at what ratio.

Last updated 25 Sep 2026

What is a CEDEAR?

A CEDEAR (Certificado de Depósito Argentino) is a certificate listed on BYMA, the Buenos Aires exchange, that represents a fixed number of shares in a foreign company. It trades in pesos, during local hours, through a local broker — so an investor in Argentina can hold exposure to a US-listed company without a US brokerage account.

The ratio is how many CEDEARs equal one underlying share. At 20:1, twenty CEDEARs represent one share, so each CEDEAR is worth roughly one twentieth of the US price, adjusted by the exchange rate the market uses to price them.

Ratios must be verified before you act on them. Ratios change with stock splits, spin-offs and issuer adjustments. Rows below marked unverified ratio are our best understanding and have not been confirmed against the current BYMA listing. Confirm with your broker before trading.
